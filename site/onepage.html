Fruit quality

What major retailers require — and how we meet it.

Supplying fruit to Australia's major retailers means meeting detailed, constantly evolving quality standards — assessed at the orchard, in the packing shed, and again at the retailer's distribution centre. These are the things that matter most, and how we deliver them, week in and week out.

01 / Eating quality

Maturity & internal quality

Brix (sugar), acidity and the Brix-to-acid ratio decide whether fruit actually tastes good. We test maturity before every season — sliced-fruit samples and documented minimum and maximum Brix readings — so eating quality is right from the first delivery.

02 / Sizing

Size & grading

Retailers buy to precise size and count specifications. We grade to those ranges and hold them consistently — so the carton matches the spec, and the spec matches the shelf.

03 / Appearance

Defects & presentation

External quality is judged against defined major and minor defect tolerances — blemish, marking, shape and colour. Our grading is built around those tolerances to keep rejections to a minimum.

04 / Standards

Quality attribute standards

Each retailer maintains its own quality attribute standards and reviews them continually. We work hand-in-hand with retail quality teams to meet — and stay ahead of — these evolving specifications.

05 / Freshness

Cold chain & shelf life

Fruit is perishable and date-sensitive. Temperature-controlled handling and disciplined despatch protect condition and shelf life all the way to the distribution centre.

06 / Traceability

Specs, labelling & traceability

Correct product specifications, tray labels, barcodes (GTIN) and lot traceability — accurate every time, so product flows through the DC without holds or queries.

Certification, not promises

Certifications, explained

No single certification covers everything. Together, these do.

HACCP covers food safety processes. FRESHCARE covers farm-to-packing food safety and sustainability. HARPS covers retailer-specific requirements. SEDEX covers ethical trading. Together they meet the requirements of Australian retailers, humanitarian agencies, government bodies and institutional buyers across every dimension they evaluate.

HACCP

Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points

A globally recognised food safety management system — and a legal requirement for Australian food businesses. Every stage of our supply chain, from supplier qualification to packing, cold-chain and export documentation, has documented control points with monitoring and record-keeping. For institutional buyers, HACCP is the minimum baseline. We have it.

FRESHCARE

Food Safety & Quality (FSQ4.2) + Supply Chain (SCS2)

Australia's largest fresh-produce assurance programme, GFSI-benchmarked — so international buyers can accept our certificates without an additional audit. SCS2 covers our standalone packing and distribution operations. The Environmental Programme demonstrates sustainable stewardship of land, water and resources.

HARPS

Harmonised Australian Retailer Produce Scheme

The certification that unlocks Australian retail. HARPS is required for packing produce into retail-branded packaging for Australia's major supermarkets. We hold it — proof that we operate to retail-grade standards across food safety, quality systems and traceability.

SEDEX / SMETA

Supplier Ethical Data Exchange

Our labour standards, health and safety, environmental management and business ethics are audited against international benchmarks — and the results are shareable with buyers through the SEDEX platform. No self-declaration. One audit, accessible to UN agencies, NGOs, government buyers and retailers alike.

The evolution

Bungle changed because our customers' markets did.

Founded in 2008 by Richard Permewan as BungleFresh — a fresh produce exporter and domestic marketer. Over 15+ years, as our customers moved into the world's most demanding operating environments, we expanded our offer to match.

2008
Founded as BungleFresh — fresh produce export and domestic marketing, with owned farms and a Mildura juice factory.
2011
Major Australian retail citrus supply relationship established.
Mid 2010s
PNG operations formalised; in-country import company established in Boroko, NCD. Mildura farms and juice factory sold to a local family in 2015 — focus shifts fully to export and distribution.
Growth period
Product range extended into frozen food, dairy and FMCG to serve catering and mining customers.
Last 18 months
Added our own packing operation — deepening supply chain integration.
2025 / 2026
Operating under the Bungle Group brand with new proprietary software built specially for Bungle and our customers; UN Global Marketplace registered; positioned for government and institutional procurement across the Pacific.
// founder-led since 2008 Richard Permewan — Founder, Bungle Group

Founder-led

The people who run this business have their name on it.

Richard Permewan founded Bungle in 2008, building it from a single produce export venture into a multi-market food supply operation with staff across Australia and PNG. The business operates across several related entities — Bungle Group (export & supply chain), I Am Thirsty (dehydrated fruit and cocktail garnish), and Vantage Pacific (insurance broking for Pacific trade).

We are privately owned. No private equity pressure to cut corners, no offshore parent, no conflicting interests. In an industry where suppliers come and go, longevity is integrity made visible — our oldest customer relationships predate our youngest staff members.
